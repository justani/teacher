# Learner Tutoring Session: Equal Exterior Angles

## Problem

In the figure, points S, Q, R, and T lie on a straight line. Given that
∠PQR = ∠PRQ, prove that ∠PQS = ∠PRT.

## Conversation

**Me:** kya puch raha he question?

**She:** prove karna he k angle PQS equal to angle PRT he.

**Me:** aur kya diya hua he?

**She:** k angle PQR = angle PRQ he.

**Me:** ab kese solve kare fir ise?

**She:** nahi pata

**Me:** socho k angle PQS aur PQR me kya relation he?

**She:** linear pair he

**Me:** to kya PQR se PQS nikal sakte he

*[She did not understand this. I had to teach her equality of angles and how
one angle can be expressed using another in an abstract form, without knowing
either angle's actual value.]*

**Me:** to finally kya hui PQS ki value?

**She:** PQS = 180 - PQR

**Me:** fir ese hi kya hum PRT bhi nikal skte he

**She:** yes PRT = 180 - PRQ

**Me:** perfect, ab kya kare

**She:** nahi pata

**Me:** ek kaam karo subtract kro, subtract krte aati he equations?

**She:** haan, PQS - PRT = 180 - PQR - (180 - PRQ)

*[I then had to remind her that PQR = PRQ is already given. Substituting that
gives PQS - PRT = 0, and therefore PQS = PRT.]*

## What this session showed

- She correctly read both the goal and the given information from the question.
- She independently recognized ∠PQS and ∠PQR as a linear pair.
- She did not initially understand how to represent one angle in terms of
  another when no numerical values were known.
- After that idea was taught, she could form both supplementary-angle
  equations.
- She could subtract the equations when prompted, but needed a reminder to use
  the given equality and finish the proof.
- This session did not test whether she could independently use the same idea
  on a new problem.
